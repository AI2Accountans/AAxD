Thanks for this - and congrats on getting the Taller 1 pipeline over the line. I read through it with real interest.

On XBRL GL: I think we're aiming at the same destination - self-describing, self-reconciling transactional holons - from different roads, and I want to be straight with you about mine. I capture the ledger relationally at Moment 0 and materialize it into the LPG graph, so the holon is the native artifact; I don't round-trip through an intermediate serialization to get there. I can emit JSON-LD (or XBRL) at the report boundary, and I can include the events named graph linked to the facts it aggregates up to - but by default I don't, because most reports (a 10-K being the extreme case) are summaries, and the linkage from facts back to the ledger isn't meant to survive publication.

So for my architecture, XBRL GL as a required internal bridge doesn't earn its place as the events ledger materializes into the graph.

Where I'd love to compare notes is the mapping question. Your UBL to GL to JSON-LD flow is elegant, but the step where you inject the accounting semantics into the GL instance is the interesting one to me. A UBL invoice carries commercial semantics - who, what, amount, tax - but the accounting semantics (which accounts, the debit/credit treatment, the accrual timing) aren't in the source; they're a judgment applied on top. That mapping layer is exactly what I've tried to make structural rather than manual, so I'm curious where you anchor it in your flow.

Where I actually see the durable collaboration isn't in the plumbing - our stacks won't merge, and they don't need to. It's one layer up: the frameworks themselves. I build reporting frameworks as versioned, semantically-enriched packages - the XBRL-classic layers (concepts, labels, references, presentation, calculation) plus the ones no ratified standard covers yet: type-subtype, traits, named disclosures, disclosure mechanics, the reporting checklist, rules, reporting styles - and much of that second group descends from Charlie's Seattle Method. It's all public, governed by a single ontology that inherits XBRL's vocabulary wherever XBRL has a term and mints new ones only where it genuinely lacks one; a published report comes out as one JSON-LD holon that validates against it. The idea I keep coming back to: if those frameworks are canonical and shareable, your stack and mine can each consume them their own way and still produce mutually legible holons. Shared publication model, sovereign consumption - neither of us concedes anything about engines. That's the layer I'd most want to build toward with you.

There's frontier stuff I'd enjoy kicking around live too - the reciprocal-commitment layer above the event, where neither of us has a first-class contract yet - but the framework layer is the one with real staying power. Either way, I'd be glad to contribute one of my real-data holons to the A&AD repo as a concrete starting point.
