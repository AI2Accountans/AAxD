Central Question
Charlie Hoffman's starting point for this analysis

What is the relationship between UBL, REA (ISO/IEC), ACTUS, FASB/IFRS Taxonomies, SBVR, BPMN, and XBRL?
How are all these standards related to each other?

✅ They are not competitors — they are layers of a single semantic supply chain for business meaning.
"All of these standards form a single semantic supply chain for business meaning; from the moment a business event occurs, through operational processing, into accounting recognition, and finally into regulated financial compliance reporting."

Layer 1 — Conceptual, Semantic and Logical Foundation
Defining meaning, concepts, and rules
CONCEPTUAL
SBVR
Semantics of Business Vocabulary and Business Rules
OMG Standard · v1.5 Ratified

The glue of the entire stack. Provides a formal, language-independent way to define business terms and rules.

  • Defines the meaning of UBL document elements
  • Describes REA and ACTUS accounting concepts
  • Formalizes XBRL GL and XBRL Reporting contents
  • Supports governance and epistemic risk management
  • Bridge between Business People ↔ Technical People
Since the year 2000
↗ omg.org/spec/SBVR
BPMN
Business Process Model and Notation
OMG Standard · Ratified

The temporal dimension. Represents business workflows through which business event information travels.

  • Models the when and how information flows
  • Complements SBVR (which defines the what it means)
  • Orchestrates the processes that generate and consume all other standards
↗ omg.org/bpmn
⚙️
Layer 2 — Operational Workflow and Document Layer
Describing action, execution, and payload
OPERATIONAL
UBL
Universal Business Language
ISO/IEC 66370 · Ratified

The natively semantic source document. Structured documents (invoices, orders, dispatch advices) describing real-world commercial interactions.

  • Carries granular facts: who, what, when, why
  • The document IS the event — no manual mapping needed
  • Preserves meaning across all operational steps
  • HEADER pattern (who, when) + DETAIL (line items, amounts, taxes)
🔑 Shift Left — source of truth
↗ iso.org/standard/66370
ACTUS
Algorithmic Contract Types Unified Standards
On path to ISO/IEC · ACTUS FRF

The financial state machine. Models financial contracts as deterministic state machines.

  • Covers: loans, derivatives, bonds
  • Generates discrete future cash-flow business events
  • Financial institution (side 1) ↔ Enterprise (side 2)
  • Unique capability: projects business events far into the future
⏰ Future projection
↗ actusfrf.org
📊
Layer 3 — Accounting & Aggregation Layer
Transacting, recording, aggregating, reporting
ACCOUNTING / REPORTING
REA
Resources, Events, Agents
ISO/IEC 67199 · Accounting & Economic Ontology

The accounting semantics. Interprets UBL documents as economic events with ontological meaning.

  • Transforms the commercial document into an accounting event
  • Answers: which account? debit/credit? accrual timing?
  • Provides the framework that gives accounting meaning to the UBL payload
💡 Answers Joey's question
↗ iso.org/standard/67199
XBRL GL
XBRL Global Ledger
XBRL International · Ratified

The immutable bridge. Universal audit trail and internal reporting standard.

  • Ingests: UBL invoices, ACTUS cash flows, ERP systems
  • Provides full structural provenance
  • Links back to the originating operational event
  • Foundation for mapping to compliance reports
🔒 Universal audit trail
↗ xbrl.org — Global Ledger
XBRL + Taxonomies
XBRL Financial Reporting + FASB US GAAP / IFRS Taxonomies
XBRL International + FASB + IFRS Foundation · Current

The top of the pipeline. External regulatory compliance reporting, standardized.

UBL Documents
  → XBRL GL journal entries
    → Aggregation per US GAAP / IFRS
      → Mapping to official XBRL Taxonomies
        → External financial statements + regulatory filings
  • Reports the "state" and "changes in state" of the economic entity
  • Organized per the standard reporting framework (US GAAP / IFRS)

The Copper Plumbing Metaphor
The economic argument for "doing it right from the start"
📽 Cosmo Castorini (plumber) in Moonstruck:
"... Then there is copper. It costs money. It costs money. Because it saves money."

Charlie applies the metaphor directly to accounting system architecture:

"When I build my accounting information systems, I use the equivalent of copper 'plumbing'. The initial investment to do it right is higher; but things work better, the quality is higher, and things last significantly longer."
→ The real value is no longer just a well-functioning accounting system. It is maximizing the benefit and utility of artificial intelligence.

Self-Inflicted Complexity
Problems organizations create for themselves — all avoidable
ProblemDiagnosisReference
Accidental complexityEmerges from poor design decisions, not from the nature of the problem—
KludgePatches and workarounds that accumulate until the system becomes unmanageable↗ No Kludge
Physical fragmentationThe same data in multiple systems with no interoperability↗ Fragmentation
Semantic fragmentationThe same concepts with different meanings across different systems↗ Fragmentation
The "hairball"Entangled system where transformations leave the problem the same or worse↗ Creeping Normality
"If your accounting information is a hairball and then you go through a transformation and things are not much better on the other end; the only thing you will have achieved is waste your hard earned money."

Alignment Notes — DFRNT / AI2Accountans Project
How the blog connects directly with what we have already built

🔗 The pipeline we built maps onto this architecture

The pipeline demonstrated in UBL2XBRLGL_CSV.zip is exactly the Operational → Accounting layer Charlie describes:

  • UBL (Operational Layer) → natively semantic source document
  • XBRL GL (Accounting Layer) → immutable bridge, audit trail
  • JSON-LD (Projection) → implementation of the XBRL OIM that Noble described

🎯 REA answers Joey's question

Joey asked: "The accounting semantics (which accounts, debit/credit, timing) are not in the UBL — where do you anchor them?"

Charlie answers implicitly in this blog: it is REA (ISO/IEC 67199) that transforms the commercial document into an economic event with accounting semantics. This is the layer that still needs to be explicitly documented in our pipeline.

🧠 SBVR bridges the two tribes

SBVR was designed precisely for the problem Charlie described in the Jul 26 mail: the bridge between Business People and Technical People.

"The vision of SBVR has always been to enable the people who manage and run an organization to be able to express their business policies, business rules, and other governance documentation unambiguously using the same natural language and vocabulary they use every day — in a standards-based, machine-readable format that has an interpretation in formal logic."

This is what the A&AD framework needs for adoption: rules expressed in business language, formally interpretable by machine.
